The font you choose shapes how your Quran video feels. The right font makes verses look sacred, elegant, and easy to read. The wrong one makes them hard to follow, even if everything else is perfect. Choosing among the many arabic fonts quran videos can use is easier once you know what to look for, so this guide covers the best options and the readability rules that matter most.

## What Makes a Good Quran Font

A font for Quran videos needs to do more than look pretty. It must handle:

- **Proper Arabic joining:** Arabic letters change shape depending on their position in a word. A good font joins them correctly and smoothly.
- **Diacritics and marks:** Quranic text includes vowel marks and recitation symbols. The font must render these clearly and in the right place, not overlapping the letters.
- **Readability at video sizes:** Text on video is read from a distance and on small phone screens. Thin, decorative strokes that look fine in print can disappear on video.
- **A calm, respectful feel:** Quran videos suit fonts that feel classical and dignified rather than playful or futuristic.

Test any font at the actual size you will use in the video, on a phone screen, before committing to it. A font that looks great in a large preview can be unreadable when it is smaller.

## Classic Quranic Fonts

These fonts are modelled on traditional Quranic calligraphy and are the safest choices for recitation videos.

### Amiri

Amiri is one of the most widely used fonts for Quranic text. It is based on the Bulaq Press style and has a classical, book-like feel with clear letterforms and well-placed diacritics. It reads beautifully at large sizes and is a strong default choice for any Quran video. If you are unsure where to start, start with Amiri.

### Scheherazade New

Scheherazade New is designed specifically for Arabic script with full support for Quranic marks. Its letters are open and clear, which makes it very readable on screens, including smaller ones. It is a good choice when your audience includes new readers or viewers watching on phones.

### Lateef

Lateef is a clean Naskh-style font with generous spacing. It feels lighter than Amiri and works well for videos where you also show a translation, because the Arabic does not overpower the rest of the screen. It is a good middle ground between traditional and modern.

## Clean Modern Options

If you want a slightly more contemporary look without losing readability, these fonts are worth trying.

### Noto Naskh Arabic

Part of Google's Noto family, Noto Naskh Arabic is a reliable, well-tested font with broad language support. It is less ornate than Amiri, which suits minimal video styles and clean backgrounds. Its consistency across devices is a practical advantage.

### Harmattan

Harmattan is designed for readability on screens and has a friendly, open feel. It works well for short-form videos and social media, where viewers read quickly and move on. If you make [Quran reels](/blog/quran-reels-instagram-tiktok-guide), a font like Harmattan keeps the text legible even at the smaller sizes vertical video requires.

### Reem Kufi

Reem Kufi is a geometric Kufi-style font. It is best used for titles, headings, and short phrases rather than long passages, because Kufi letterforms are more decorative and slower to read in bulk. Use it for your video title or channel branding, and pair it with a Naskh font for the verses themselves.

## Readability Tips That Matter More Than the Font

Even the best font fails if the styling is wrong. These rules matter as much as the font choice:

- **Size:** Make the Arabic text as large as your layout allows. On a phone, viewers should be able to read comfortably without zooming.
- **Contrast:** Light text on a dark background, or dark text on a light background, with strong contrast between the two. Avoid text over busy images.
- **Line spacing:** Give the lines room to breathe. Cramped lines make diacritics blur together.
- **One verse at a time:** Showing one verse per screen is easier to follow than a full page of text, especially on phones.
- **Highlight colour:** When a verse is being read, highlight it in a warm, gentle colour that stands out without clashing with the background.

The background also plays a role. A calm, simple background lets the font shine, while a detailed photo fights with it. Our guide to [Quran video backgrounds](/blog/quran-video-backgrounds-ideas) covers pairings that keep the text readable.

## Pairing Fonts With Your Video Style

Think of your font as part of your channel's identity. A traditional style suits Amiri or Scheherazade New with a mosque or gradient background. A modern, minimal style suits Noto Naskh Arabic or Lateef with a plain dark background. A social-media style suits Harmattan with bold, large text.

Whatever you choose, stay consistent. Using the same font across your videos makes your channel recognisable, and viewers learn to read your style quickly. Changing fonts every video forces them to adjust each time.

## Testing Before You Publish

Before you settle on a font, run this quick test:

1. Render a short sample with your chosen font, size, and background.
2. Watch it on your phone, on a laptop, and if possible on a television.
3. Ask someone else to read the text from a normal viewing distance.
4. Check that the diacritics are clear and nothing overlaps.

If it passes on all three screens, you have a winner. If it only looks good on the laptop, increase the size or switch to a more open font.

Start with Amiri or Scheherazade New for a classic look, try Noto Naskh Arabic or Lateef for a cleaner style, and save decorative Kufi fonts for titles.
